📜 Text: "Homeless Hope"

(1) Eduardo Drio, aged 16, does voluntary work with street children in his home city in Manila, the capital of the Philippines. There are over 100 million children in the world who live on the streets because they have no home. I do voluntary work for an organisation called Homeless Hope which helps street children to get off the streets and to lead a normal life. It was founded in 1996 by cooperative teenagers.

(2) There is a big difference between street children and street-working children. Street-working children make money by selling sweets and things. Then they go home. But street children have no home. They live and work on the streets. They sleep on the pavement and in bus shelters. They beg, steal or sell things to make a living.

(3) Here in Manila, our organisation found an area where there were lots of street children. We talked to about sixty of them and gave them food and first aid. Slowly they began to trust us and we persuaded them to come to our hostel to eat and sleep. Most of the kids won't tell us about their backgrounds. But if they want to talk to us about their families and where they come from, we'll listen.

(4) We've also started an education programme. If kids learn a trade, they'll have a chance of finding work. We want them to make something of their lives.

📘 Detailed Correction – 9th – In-depth Analysis

I- Reading Comprehension (6 marks) – Correction & Analysis

1. Tick the right option: 1 mark

Answer:

The text is about an organization that helps homeless children. The very first lines state: "street children in his home city" and "over 100 million children in the world who live on the streets because they have no home." The organisation is called Homeless Hope, which clearly indicates its mission.

🔍 In-depth Analysis: This question tests global comprehension of the text's main theme. The key lexical field revolves around "street children", "no home", "live on the streets". The name of the NGO itself—Homeless Hope—is a lexical clue that directly points to the correct answer. The student must distinguish between "needy" (a broader term) and "homeless" (specific). The question also implicitly checks vocabulary: understanding that "street children" are children without a home, not merely poor children. This is a fundamental reading skill: identifying the central topic through title, repeated nouns, and the opening paragraph.

Answer:

🔍 In-depth Analysis: This question requires scanning the text for specific factual information. The name of the organisation appears in paragraph (1): "an organisation called Homeless Hope". The location is given at the very beginning: "in his home city in Manila, the capital of the Philippines". The skill tested is selective reading—the ability to locate precise details without being distracted by the surrounding narrative. The table format encourages organised note-taking, an essential academic competence. The answer "Manila" alone is acceptable, but adding "the Philippines" shows full retrieval.

3. Does the organisation give lessons to the children? 1 mark

Answer:

Yes.
Justification: "We've also started an education programme. If kids learn a trade, they'll have a chance of finding work." (Paragraph 4)

🔍 In-depth Analysis: This is an inferential question: the word "lessons" is not explicitly mentioned, but the phrase "education programme" and "learn a trade" imply teaching and skill-building. The student must understand that an "education programme" necessarily includes lessons or training sessions. The conditional structure ("If kids learn a trade") reinforces the idea that the organisation is actively teaching them something. This question evaluates the ability to connect synonyms and to paraphrase: "give lessons" = "education programme" = "learn a trade". It also tests the recognition of the present perfect ("We've started") as indicating a current, relevant initiative.

4. Find a word meaning "to have confidence in someone": 1 mark

Answer:

trust (from paragraph 3: "Slowly they began to trust us")

🔍 In-depth Analysis: This vocabulary-in-context question asks the student to locate a synonym. The definition "to have confidence in someone" is a clear paraphrase of the verb "trust". The context—street children gradually opening up to volunteers after receiving food and aid—illustrates the process of building trust. The word "trust" is a central concept in the text, reflecting the emotional barrier between the children and the helpers. Mastering this question requires the ability to match definitions to words within a paragraph, a key lexicographic skill.

5. What does "them" refer to? 1 mark

Answer:

"them" refers to the kids / the street children.

🔍 In-depth Analysis: Pronoun reference is a classic reading comprehension test. In paragraph 4, "We want them to make something of their lives", the pronoun "them" anaphorically refers back to the last mentioned plural noun group—the children the organisation helps. The student must trace the discourse thread: "kids" in paragraph 3, then "them" in paragraph 4. This seemingly simple exercise is crucial for understanding textual cohesion. Misinterpreting the referent could lead to a complete misunderstanding of the organisation's target group. It also reinforces awareness of how writers avoid repetition through pronominalisation.

II- Language (8 marks) – Correction & Grammar Rules

1- Circle the correct option. 2.5 marks

Answers:

• Why don't we first visit (infinitive after "Why don't we")
• There we can taste exotic food. (can + base form; "taste" matches food context)
• That sounds great! (sense verb + adjective, expressing opinion)
• visiting the zoo (clue: "very unique animals")
• I'm not really interested in going there. (be + interested + in + -ing form)

✔ Grammar Justification:
• Why don't we + base verb: fixed structure for making suggestions.
• can + base verb: modal expressing possibility; "taste" is the logical verb for food.
• That sounds + adjective: linking verb "sound" used to give an impression; "sounds great" is a common collocation.
• zoo: contextual clue "unique animals" directly points to a zoo, not a funfair or theatre.
• be interested in: adjective phrase; "interested" describes the person's feeling, while "interesting" describes the thing causing the feeling.

2. wonderful (adjective, exclamation: How wonderful!)
3. have never been (present perfect: experience up to now with "never")

✔ Tense & Form Rules:
• since + past simple: "It's a long time since we met" uses past simple to refer to the last occurrence.
• How + adjective! exclamatory structure; "wonder" becomes "wonderful".
• never + present perfect: "I have never been" expresses a life experience up to the present.
• don't mind + gerund: "mind" is followed by the -ing form when it means "object to".
• will + base verb: spontaneous decision or future intention expressed with "will".

III- Writing – Article (6 marks) – Plan, Model & Analysis

Task: Write an article about the importance of volunteering and encourage others to help needy people.

📌 Expected Article Structure

  • Engaging title: e.g., "Together We Can Change the World"
  • Introduction: hook the reader, define volunteering briefly.
  • Body paragraph 1: why volunteering matters (personal growth, community impact).
  • Body paragraph 2: concrete ways young people can help (examples).
  • Conclusion: powerful call to action, encouraging readers to start.

📝 Model Article (expected level):

Together We Can Change the World Have you ever thought about how a few hours of your time could transform someone's life? Volunteering is not just about giving; it is about growing, learning, and connecting with those who need a helping hand. In our fast-paced world, dedicating time to others is one of the most powerful actions a young person can take. Firstly, volunteering builds bridges between people. When you help someone in need—whether by distributing food, tutoring a child, or simply listening—you create a bond of solidarity. You discover that the world is bigger than your own concerns, and you develop empathy, patience, and gratitude. Moreover, volunteering strengthens communities from within: it shows that we do not have to wait for governments or big organisations to act; we can be the change ourselves. There are countless ways for students to get involved. You can join a local charity, participate in a clean-up campaign, visit elderly people, or collect clothes and books for families who struggle. Even small gestures matter. Remember the story of Homeless Hope: teenagers like us founded an organisation that now saves street children. If they could do it, so can we. So, what are you waiting for? Volunteering is not about being a hero; it is about being human. Start today. Offer your time, your skills, your smile. Together, step by step, we can indeed change the world—one act of kindness at a time. — By a student who believes in you
✔ Key strengths of this model: The article has a clear, engaging title and a compelling introduction with a rhetorical question. The body develops two distinct arguments: the personal and social benefits of volunteering, and practical suggestions for young people. The reference to the text's organisation (Homeless Hope) shows an ability to link ideas across the test. The conclusion is an energetic call to action with an inspiring tone. The language is appropriate for a school magazine: accessible, persuasive, and grammatically varied. Connectives ("Firstly", "Moreover", "So") ensure smooth progression of ideas.

Marking criteria – Writing (6 marks):
• Adherence to task & content (2 marks): clear article format, all points addressed, persuasive purpose.
• Lexical appropriacy & grammar accuracy (2 marks): varied vocabulary, correct tenses, sentence structures.
• Mechanical accuracy (2 marks): spelling, punctuation, paragraphing.
